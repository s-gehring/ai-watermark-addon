# How it works

## Which images are checked

- `<img>` elements and CSS background images whose shorter rendered side is at least 200 px (configurable)
- images inside open and closed shadow roots (web components), also nested ones
- images loaded later as well (lazy loading, infinite scroll, `srcset` changes); an image is checked only once it gets close to the visible area
- SVGs are skipped

## Pipeline

The background script fetches each image again (usually from the HTTP cache), so images from other domains can be checked without a CORS header. Metadata scan, decoding, the SynthID check and inference run in a Web Worker to keep the popup and background script responsive. Results are cached per URL. Images are checked one after another, visible ones first; one image takes about 0.8 s in Firefox.

Scaling and recompression remove C2PA manifests and SynthID patterns, so if the page offers a larger version of an image, the metadata and watermark checks read that file instead. In this order the add-on takes the original behind a known thumbnail URL (Wikimedia, Reddit, WordPress, Google's image hosting and the WebP renditions of Google's blogs, where it tries PNG and then JPEG), an image file the image links to, or the largest `srcset` candidate if it is at least 1.25 times as large. Links and `srcset` candidates count only if they come from the origin of the image or of the page. The larger file is used only if it has the same aspect ratio (±2 %) and is at least as large; the model still scores the displayed image. Larger files load two at a time, at most 16 MB each and 20 per page. CSS background images only get the known thumbnail URLs.

Each image goes through up to three checks. The first one that finds something decides the badge.

## 1. Metadata

Badge "AI · Metadata". A metadata hit is treated as certain. Detected labels:

- IPTC Digital Source Type `trainedAlgorithmicMedia` or `compositeWithTrainedAlgorithmicMedia` in XMP or in C2PA manifests (OpenAI, Google, Adobe Firefly, Midjourney, Amazon Bedrock and others)
- Stable Diffusion generation parameters (A1111/Forge), ComfyUI graphs (the `prompt` chunk with sampler, loader or text encoder nodes, the `workflow` chunk, or the same JSON in the EXIF user comment), NovelAI, InvokeAI
- EXIF tags of Grok (`ImageDescription: Signature: <base64>` in original JPEG downloads) and Ideogram (`Make: Ideogram AI`), Reve (`Software: Reve.art`) and the on-site generator of Civitai (`Artist: ai`)
- metadata from the Stability AI API: the EXIF fields `Model: stability-diffusion` and `Software: stability.ai` (SDXL on Amazon Bedrock, v1 API) and the C2PA manifest of Stability's Content Credentials service, which carries no IPTC source type
- the AIGC label required by the Chinese labeling standard GB 45438-2025 (JSON with `Label`, `ContentProducer`, `ProduceID` and so on, as PNG text chunk `AIGC`, in the XMP namespace `http://www.tc260.org.cn/ns/AIGC/1.0/` or in the EXIF comment; mandatory for providers in China since September 2025, for example Kling)

If a C2PA manifest declares the SynthID watermark (Google writes "Applied imperceptible SynthID watermark"), the badge reads "AI · SynthID".

Many platforms strip metadata on upload. Then only the next two checks remain.

## 2. SynthID watermark

Badge "AI · SynthID". Google embeds the invisible [SynthID](https://deepmind.google/models/synthid/) watermark in images from Gemini and Imagen, and since May 2026 OpenAI does the same for all images from ChatGPT and its API. There is no public detector.

The add-on therefore checks for a fixed component of the watermark that can be measured in watermarked images: at certain spatial frequencies, counted in cycles per image width and height, the spectrum of every watermarked image has the same phase, regardless of the image content. The image is scaled to 512 × 512 or 1024 × 1024 and split into YCbCr channels. Each pixel is then replaced by its deviation from the mean of its 3 × 3 neighborhood, divided by the standard deviation there plus 0.5, so that textured regions do not drown the faint pattern and flat regions do not stand out. Each channel is transformed with an FFT, and the phases are compared with templates averaged over watermarked images ([`src/background/synthid-templates/`](../src/background/synthid-templates/), [`synthid.json`](../synthid.json)). The result is a z-score per template, roughly standard normal for images without the watermark. From 10 (Google) or 8 (OpenAI, Amazon) on, the watermark counts as found, the model is skipped, and the details panel names the provider.

- **Google:** two templates from 988 Gemini images with the same phases. The first weights each frequency by how consistently its phase recurs across the images. Recompression destroys the pattern mostly in the color channels and at high frequencies, so the second template additionally weights each band of 32 × 32 frequencies per channel by the share of the pattern that survives WebP 80 and downscaling to 1280 or 800 px with JPEG 80 or 75, measured on copies of the template images (on one half, with phases from the other). It relies mainly on brightness and lower frequencies and still finds many images that were recompressed for the web. Because frequencies are counted relative to the image size, both work at any resolution and aspect ratio.
- **OpenAI:** one template for every output size and aspect ratio. The luma channel carries the same phases in all output sizes that ChatGPT and the API produce (measured in 14 sizes from 1055 × 1491 to 1983 × 793, among them 1024 × 1536, 1536 × 1024 and 1254 × 1254), because frequencies are counted relative to the image size. The template is built from 2,034 images with a verified watermark, up to 200 per output size (the files are pinned in [`tools/synthid/openai_sources.json`](../tools/synthid/openai_sources.json)). Each frequency is averaged only over the images that can show it at their own resolution. The template uses only the luma channel, because the color channels differ between output sizes, and masks a 2 % border, because otherwise edge effects instead of the watermark would end up in the template. It exists twice: at 512 × 512 it shares the spectrum of the Google template and costs almost nothing, at 1024 × 1024 (up to 384 cycles per image) it is finer, but the 1024 × 1024 luma spectrum is only computed for images where the 512 template reaches z 3. That keeps it off almost all images without the watermark.
- **Amazon Nova Canvas:** Amazon's image model on Bedrock (until September 2026) writes a fixed component of the same kind into the Cr channel at high frequencies. It differs from Google's pattern; whether it is SynthID with Amazon's own key or another scheme cannot be told from the pixels, so a hit reads "AI · Watermark" with "Amazon Nova Canvas" in the details panel. One template at 1024 × 1024 for all sizes and aspect ratios uses only the Cr channel above 128 cycles per image and masks a 2 % border. It is built from 75 Nova Canvas PNGs from 10 GitHub repositories whose C2PA manifest names the model. Images whose Cr channel hardly varies (standard deviation below 0.5 after scaling, as in black-and-white images) are skipped, because there the phases only reflect rounding noise. Google's watermark, in contrast, is still found in the faint color of black-and-white Gemini images, so this rule applies to the Amazon template only. Images from Amazon's older Titan Image Generator share no such component across seeds and are not covered.

A crop changes the image size, so the frequencies of the pattern no longer fall where the templates expect them. For images without a hit that the model (section 3) scores at 50 % or more, the add-on therefore searches the two Google templates and the 512 × 512 OpenAI template for crops of known output sizes (53 Gemini and 22 ChatGPT sizes in both orientations, listed in [`synthid.json`](../synthid.json)). Each size gives one hypothesis: either a crop along one axis to the aspect ratio of the image (down to 30 % of that side), or, if both sides shrank by the same factor down to 75 %, a crop on all sides. For each hypothesis the template frequencies are mapped onto the 512 × 512 spectrum that is already computed, interpolating between neighboring frequencies, and an FFT finds the best crop position. Crops along one axis are found after any downscaling, crops on all sides only at the original scale. From z 8 on the badge reads "AI · SynthID". In images without the watermark the highest value over all hypotheses and positions is about 4, and at most 7.1 in 49,614 real images; a tail fit puts the chance of a false hit below 1 in 300,000 searched images.

Measured in Node, the SynthID check takes about 0.25 s for a 4-megapixel image at any aspect ratio (0.13 s of it for the Google and OpenAI templates, the rest for the 1024 × 1024 spectrum of the Cr channel that the Amazon template needs). The crop search adds about 0.1 s for each image that it searches.

[`tools/synthid/synthid.py`](../tools/synthid/synthid.py) is the reference implementation of [`src/background/synthid.ts`](../src/background/synthid.ts); a unit test checks that both produce the same z-scores. Detection rates and false positives are in [accuracy.md](accuracy.md#synthid-watermark).

### Stable Diffusion, SDXL and FLUX watermark

After the SynthID templates, the same step decodes the classic [invisible-watermark](https://pypi.org/project/invisible-watermark) (`dwtDct`) that the Stable Diffusion reference scripts (`StableDiffusionV1`, `SDV2`), SDXL (a fixed 48-bit pattern, written by the pipelines of Stability and diffusers and by the Stability API and Amazon Bedrock) and the FLUX reference code of Black Forest Labs (another fixed 46-bit pattern) write into the pixels. It is a fixed algorithm without a secret key: a Haar transform of the U channel, in each 4 × 4 block the largest coefficient modulo 36 gives one bit, and the bits are voted per payload position. diffusers before April 2024 handed RGB images to the encoder, which expects BGR, so the decoder also tries the U channel with red and blue swapped. A hit needs the decoded bits to match a payload at z ≥ 5 (about 1 false positive in 1.4 million images with random bits; none in 9,090 images without it). Badge "AI · Watermark". Detection rates are in [accuracy.md](accuracy.md#stable-diffusion-sdxl-and-flux-watermark).

This only works on images that are still byte-identical to the generator output: the scheme does not survive JPEG compression (not even at quality 90), scaling or cropping, and many front ends (A1111, ComfyUI, Forge) switch it off. In practice it only matches PNGs straight from a local pipeline or from the SDXL API.

### ByteDance tile pattern

Doubao and Jimeng (ByteDance) add a faint pattern to the Cb channel that repeats every 112 × 96 pixels from the top left corner. The add-on folds the Cb channel of images with at least 24 whole tiles onto one tile, removes the local mean and correlates the result with the pattern averaged over flat black and gray calibration images from [pagedMark](https://github.com/doofzoff/pagedMark) ([`src/background/bytedance-tile.json`](../src/background/bytedance-tile.json)). From a correlation of 0.5 on, the badge reads "AI · Watermark" and the details panel names ByteDance. Watermarked images score 0.86 to 0.89, images without the pattern at most 0.16. The check survives JPEG compression down to about quality 80 at the original size, but not scaling or cropping, and the Seedream API and images downloaded without the watermark do not carry it. Detection rates are in [accuracy.md](accuracy.md#bytedance-tile-pattern).

### Visible Gemini logo

If no invisible watermark is found, the same step looks for the white four-pointed star that Gemini puts into the bottom right corner of generated images. Badge "AI · Watermark", and the details panel names the visible watermark and Gemini. Since August 2026 users can switch the logo off, reportedly except in countries that require a visible AI label. SynthID usually no longer survives in images that are shared with the logo (8 of 34 in the test sets), so the logo is often the only pixel evidence left.

Gemini blends the logo onto the image as α · 255 + (1 − α) · original with a fixed alpha map. Size and distance to the corner depend on the output size: in the older layout the logo is 48 px with a margin of 32 px (96 px and 64 px if both sides exceed 1024 px), in the newer one 36 to 96 px with a margin of twice its size. Downscaled copies shrink both. The check therefore tries logo sizes from 16 to 96 px in steps of 5 % for both layouts (margin 2/3 or 2 times the logo size) and a search window of ±1/12 of the logo size around the expected position. For each candidate it averages the corner into a grid in which the logo is 24 px wide and compares it with the alpha map by normalized cross-correlation. A correlation of at least 0.88 counts as found.

Two details from the blending model make this work on more backgrounds than a plain grayscale comparison:

- The logo multiplies the distance to white, 255 − value, by 1 − α in every channel. After a logarithm the logo adds the same pattern on dark and bright parts of the background, so a logo that straddles an edge still matches.
- The check uses the darkest channel (the minimum of red, green and blue), where a white logo stands out most on colored backgrounds, and removes a linear brightness gradient before correlating.

The alpha map comes from [GeminiWatermarkTool](https://github.com/allenk/GeminiWatermarkTool) (MIT). Both layouts use the same star and differ only in opacity, which the correlation ignores, so the add-on ships a single map, downscaled to 24 × 24 px ([`src/background/gemini-logo.json`](../src/background/gemini-logo.json), 576 bytes). The check reads only the bottom right corner and takes about 3 ms per image in Node, independent of the resolution. [`tools/visible/gemini_logo.py`](../tools/visible/gemini_logo.py) is the reference implementation of [`src/background/visible-watermark.ts`](../src/background/visible-watermark.ts); a unit test checks that both produce the same score. Detection rates are in [accuracy.md](accuracy.md#visible-gemini-logo).

### Visible Kling logo

If neither an invisible watermark nor the Gemini logo is found, the same step looks for the white logo that Kling (Kuaishou) puts into the bottom right corner of images from its free tier. Badge "AI · Watermark", and the details panel names the visible watermark and Kling. Many current Kling images also carry the AIGC label (see [Metadata](#1-metadata)), but platforms often strip it, and Kling images from 2024 carry none, so the logo is often the only evidence left.

The check knows two layouts, both measured on real images:

- **2024:** a round symbol and the text "KLING AI", about 8.4 % of the long side wide (86 px at 1024 × 1024), 2.3 % of the long side away from the right edge and 1.9 % from the bottom edge.
- **current (2026):** an outlined symbol and the text "KlingAI", about 19.3 % of the short side wide (296 px at 2720 × 1536). The distance to the right edge is 1.65 % of the long side, but at most 2.9 % of the short side (45 px at 2720 × 1536), the distance to the bottom edge about 0.7 times that.

Size and position scale with the image, so downscaled copies keep the proportions and the check needs no wide size search. Per layout it computes the expected box from the image size, tries five scales from −4 % to +4 % in steps of 2 % and a search window of ±2 grid cells in half-cell steps, averages the box into a grid of 48 × 12 (2024) or 64 × 18 cells (current) and correlates it with the logo after the same transformation as the Gemini check: darkest channel, −log(255 + 8 − value), linear brightness gradient removed. The half-cell steps matter because the strokes of the text are only about one cell wide. A correlation of at least 0.6 counts as found. Logos narrower than 24 px are not searched.

Unlike Gemini's half-transparent star, Kling's logo is nearly opaque white with soft edges, so there is no alpha map to take over. The add-on instead ships the average contrast of the logo in this feature, measured on the real images: the mean grid at the expected position over 1,005 images from 2024 and 68 current images, minus the background level, with values below 5 % of the maximum set to zero ([`src/background/kling-logo.json`](../src/background/kling-logo.json), 1,728 bytes). Averaged over that many backgrounds, only the logo remains. The check takes 2.5 to 5.3 ms per image in Node, depending on the size of the logo. [`tools/visible/kling_logo.py`](../tools/visible/kling_logo.py) is the reference implementation and rebuilds the templates from the labeled images; a unit test checks that both produce the same score. Detection rates are in [accuracy.md](accuracy.md#visible-kling-logo).

### Further visible marks

If no Kling logo is found either, the same step looks for ten more marks. Badge "AI · Watermark", and the details panel names the generator.

- **Chinese labels:** since September 2025 GB 45438-2025 requires a visible AI label whose text is at least 5 % of the short side high, so these marks scale with the short side: "豆包AI生成" (Doubao, about 16 % of the short side wide), "即梦AI" with its symbol (Jimeng, 20 %), "千问AI生成" with its symbol (Qwen, 25 %) and "可灵AI 3.0" with its symbol (Kling's Chinese site, 16 %, reported as Kling). The check works like the current Kling layout: expected box from the short side, five scales from −4 % to +4 % and half-cell steps.
- **Grok:** the free tier on X draws its mark at a fixed pixel size (66 to 89 px wide, 5 to 11 px from the edges) into images of every size, in three variants: "GROK" with a slash (2024), "GROK xAI" (Aurora, January 2025) and "⊘ Grok" (since 2025). Since only the size of the original is unknown, the check scans logo widths from 4 % to 10 % of the long side in steps of 4 %, with the margins in proportion to the width, and half-cell steps. Paid plans and the API add no mark.

- **Bing Image Creator:** the "b" of Bing, about 2.9 % of the short side wide, in the bottom left corner (30 px at 1,024 × 1,024, 1.4 % of the short side from the left edge and 0.4 % from the bottom). Its template is 24 cells wide, and logos narrower than 12 px are not searched.
- **Meta AI:** the words "Meta AI" with its symbol, about 8.6 % of the short side wide, in the bottom right corner.
- **DALL·E 2:** the bar of five colored squares (yellow, cyan, green, orange, blue) in the bottom right corner, 105 × 20 px at a width of 1,024 and scaled with the width. It is no correlation: the check compares the mean color of the inner 60 % of each square with the five colors and requires the same small spread within each, at three scales from −4 % to +4 % and with up to 3 px of slack from the edges.

All other templates are 48 cells wide. Doubao and Jimeng come from the alpha maps of [remove-ai-watermarks](https://github.com/wiltodelta/remove-ai-watermarks), the others are average contrasts of real images like the Kling templates ([`src/background/visible-marks.json`](../src/background/visible-marks.json), about 8 KB). A correlation of at least 0.6 counts as found for the Chinese labels and Meta AI, 0.65 for the two current Grok marks, 0.7 for Bing and 0.8 for the 2024 Grok mark, which comes closer to random corners (up to 0.69 in images without it). [`tools/visible/marks.py`](../tools/visible/marks.py) and [`tools/visible/dalle_bar.py`](../tools/visible/dalle_bar.py) are the reference implementations; a unit test checks that both produce the same scores. Detection rates are in [accuracy.md](accuracy.md#further-visible-marks).

## 3. Model

Badge "AI 99 %". A classifier run with ONNX Runtime Web (WASM) in the worker: [Detectra fine-tuned on OpenFake](https://huggingface.co/s-gehring/detectra-openfake) (30 MB, weights quantized to 8 bits).

The starting point is [Detectra v3](https://huggingface.co/TensorFold/detectra-v1) (MIT), a fine-tune of the Community Forensics ViT-S on newer generators (DALL·E 3, Midjourney, FLUX, SD 3.5, Recraft, HiDream), on images from social networks ([WildRF](https://github.com/barcavia/RealTime-DeepfakeDetection-in-the-RealWorld)), and with real paintings, anime drawings, memes and screenshots as negatives.

With [`tools/train/`](../tools/train/) it is trained for three more epochs on 15 files from the train split of [OpenFake](https://huggingface.co/datasets/ComplexDataLab/OpenFake) (about 60,000 images, half real and half from many generators), plus about 3,500 paintings, 500 drawings and 500 prints from [The Met's Open Access collection](https://huggingface.co/datasets/metmuseum/openaccess) (CC0), 5,900 photos from COCO and charts from ChartQA as negatives. Images from the newest generators (FLUX.2, Qwen-Image, Nano Banana 2, Seedream 4 and others) are weighted higher, among them 6,768 from 319 further files of the train split, together with 1,034 Pexels photos from these files as negatives. Every training image is randomly downscaled and recompressed as JPEG so the model learns the conditions on web pages. See [development.md](development.md#train-the-model) for the training procedure.

Preprocessing: lossy WebP images without transparency are first re-encoded as JPEG with quality 89, because the training only uses JPEG compression and the model otherwise scores WebP photos and paintings too high. Then bilinear resize to 440 px, center crop to 384 px. [`src/background/preprocess.ts`](../src/background/preprocess.ts) reimplements it and matches Pillow bit for bit. Images that score between 50 % and the default threshold are scored again on center crops with a shorter edge of 576 and 768 px (at most the image's own size), and the highest score counts. At the same false positive rate this detects 2 to 6 percentage points more AI images of the measurement under web conditions and costs about 10 % more model time on photos, of which 6 to 7 % fall into the band. The raw output is calibrated with Platt scaling ([`model.json`](../model.json)); the raw value would be unsuitable as a percentage because it is often below 0.1 % for real photos.

Under web conditions, at the same false positive rate on photos, the model finds more than twice as many AI images as Community Forensics (75–77 % instead of 23 %). It flags no paintings from the Cleveland Museum of Art and 3 to 6 % of WikiArt paintings (Community Forensics: 8 to 15 % and 11 %). Details are in [accuracy.md](accuracy.md).
